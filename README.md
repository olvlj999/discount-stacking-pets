## Where people actually buy pet supplies, and what each option costs you

Chewy is the traffic leader in pet food and supplies, with Petco second and PetSmart fourth in the category's global site rankings [1]. Amazon sits outside that ranking but competes on nearly every SKU you'd care about. Practically, the differences that matter at checkout:

- **Chewy** is online-only, stocks over 100,000 products, runs its own pharmacy, and ships free on orders over $49 — but only within the contiguous US. No Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico or APO addresses [3]. In-demand items do sell out, though Chewy holds stock back for Autoship orders so subscriptions don't get stranded [3].
- **PetSmart** has roughly 1,500 physical stores, free shipping over $49, and same-day delivery through DoorDash in most areas plus curbside pickup [3]. If you've run out of food today, a store run beats a two-day wait.
- **Petco** ships free over $35, delivers in 2–5 business days with paid faster options, and supports curbside pickup plus same-day fulfilment through delivery platforms [4].
- **Local vets and grocery aisles** still win on specific lines: the cheapest tier of dry food is often cheaper at Walmart or Target, and emergency refills beat any online order [2].

The free-shipping thresholds are the quiet killer. A $38 order at Chewy costs you postage; the same cart at Petco doesn't. If your usual basket sits just under a threshold, either size up to a bag you'll use anyway or shift the order to whoever clears the bar.

## The three levers that actually change your annual spend

**Lever one: subscription discounts.** Chewy Autoship takes 5–35% off your first delivery depending on the product, then 5% off every repeat delivery of the same item [2]. Amazon's Subscribe & Save typically lands between 5% and 15%. Petco and PetSmart run their own repeat-delivery offers. Setting up a subscription is the single biggest direct discount available on pet food — and unlike a coupon code, it isn't usually a one-time trick.

**Lever two: getting frequency right.** A subscription set too tight means you're stockpiling; too loose means a panic order at retail price. ShopBack's own guidance on Chewy Autoship puts a 30lb dry dog food bag at every 6–8 weeks for a small dog, 4–5 weeks for a medium dog, and 3–4 weeks for a large one, with giant breeds stepping up to a 40lb bag [2]. Cat litter for a two-cat, two-box household cycles faster than most people assume [2]. Chewy emails 3–5 days before each delivery processes, which gives you a window to skip or push it back without penalty [2].

**Lever three: cashback on top.** This is where the platform matters. Cashback is a percentage of your eligible order returned to you after the purchase is confirmed, and on recurring pet food it compounds the same way the direct discount does.

## What voids your cashback, in order of how often it happens

These aren't edge cases. They're the standard reasons people finish a pet food order and then wonder why nothing tracked.

1. **You started somewhere else.** Opening the retailer from a bookmark, an email or a search result means the platform has no record of the visit. Every new transaction needs a fresh click-through.
2. **Ad blockers, VPNs or third-party links were active.** Facebook and Google ads, or another loyalty extension, can overwrite the tracking.
3. **You applied a code the platform doesn't list.** This voids cashback on the whole order, not just the discounted item.
4. **You left items in the cart.** For PetSmart specifically, the cart has to be empty before you click through, every time.
5. **The order was prescription or pharmacy.** Excluded at the merchant level — it won't track even if you do everything else right.
6. **You returned part of the order.** Refunds, exchanges and cancellations wipe the corresponding cashback; partial returns are pro-rated [9].
